EMBEDDED ROBOTICS / SYSTEM INTEGRATION

TSINGHUAAUG–DEC 2023 · PUBLISHED 2025

Amphibious Biomimetic Robot

Robots operating across land and water need efficient propulsion without excessive mechanical complexity. Our stingray-inspired prototype combined undulating pectoral fins with dual caudal fins to explore a compact architecture for multi-environment locomotion, with potential applications in environmental monitoring and surveying.

My contribution. Led a six-member team and implemented embedded C++ on STM32, integrating sensors, actuators, and feedback control. As first author, I contributed to conceptualization, methodology, software, experimental investigation, data curation, formal analysis, and manuscript preparation.

Design and validation. One servo drives each pectoral fin, and a fixed downward fin angle supports movement in water and on land without a separate angle-adjustment mechanism. The team evaluated propulsion using particle image velocimetry, computational fluid dynamics, and prototype tests.

SWIMMING SPEED+40%

0.10 → 0.14 m/s when caudal-fin actuation was added at a pectoral-fin frequency of 1.67 Hz.

COMBINED MODECOT 3.25

Measured cost of transport at 0.14 m/s; lower COT indicates less energy per unit weight and distance.

LAND LOCOMOTION0.06 m/s

Measured speed on ceramic tiles, with a cost of transport of 5.08.

Limitations. Experiments covered a limited speed range and controlled surfaces. Rough-terrain performance, buoyancy control, and flexible caudal fins remain future work; environmental monitoring was a potential application, not a demonstrated deployment.

BIOMECHANICS / STATE ESTIMATION

NOTRE DAME / TSINGHUAPUBLISHED 2025

Center of Mass Estimation During Non-Cyclic Activities

Estimating whole-body center of mass supports the study of human balance and informs wearable-robotics research. This study asked when a simple pelvis-marker estimate is sufficient, when whole-body tracking is needed, and whether adding ground reaction forces improves estimation.

My contribution. As co-first author, I contributed to software, methodology, investigation, data curation, formal analysis, validation, visualization, project administration, and manuscript preparation.

Methods. We analyzed an existing dataset of 10 healthy participants performing 14 activities, each repeated three times. Four estimation methods combined pelvis or whole-body markers with or without ground-reaction-force fusion using a Kalman filter. Performance was evaluated using RMS external-force residual: the mismatch between measured net force and force derived from estimated acceleration.

STUDY DESIGN10 participants

14 activities: 4 static and 10 dynamic, each repeated three times.

DYNAMIC POSITION18.40 → 9.34 N

Average position-related force residual: pelvis-based versus whole-body method groups.

DYNAMIC VELOCITY39% lower

Pelvis-based velocity-related force residual fell from 18.17 to 11.11 N with force fusion.

Findings and limitations. No statistically significant method differences were identified for static activities. Whole-body methods performed better for dynamic position estimation; force fusion improved pelvis-based velocity estimates but did not significantly improve whole-body estimates. Results are limited to 10 healthy participants and assess force residuals rather than direct center-of-mass position error.

STACK MATLAB · OpenSim · Kalman filtering · Biomechanical modeling · Statistical analysis
