EMBEDDED ROBOTICS / SYSTEM INTEGRATION

TSINGHUAAUG 2022–DEC 2023 · PUBLISHED 2025

Amphibious Biomimetic Robot

My contributionMethodsResultsDiscussion

Robots operating across land and water need efficient propulsion without excessive mechanical complexity. Our stingray-inspired prototype combined undulating pectoral fins with dual caudal fins to explore a compact architecture for multi-environment locomotion, with potential applications in environmental monitoring and surveying.

My contribution. I led a six-member team and implemented embedded C++ on STM32 to integrate sensors, actuators, and feedback control. As first author, I also contributed to the propulsion concept, experimental methods, data analysis, and manuscript preparation.

Design and evaluation

A single servo drives each pectoral fin, reducing actuator count. The fins remain tilted downward, allowing the same mechanism to propel the robot in water and contact the ground on land. Dual caudal fins provide additional underwater thrust. The team evaluated these mechanisms through flow-visualization experiments, fluid simulations, and prototype tests measuring speed, turning, and energy use.

Results

At a pectoral-fin frequency of 1.67 Hz, adding caudal-fin actuation at 1 Hz increased measured swimming speed from 0.10 to 0.14 m/s. Energy use was evaluated with cost of transport (COT): energy consumed per unit weight and distance traveled, where lower values indicate greater efficiency.

Discussion and limitations

The prototype demonstrated that one fin architecture could support locomotion in water and on land, while combined propulsion increased swimming speed under the tested conditions. Tests covered a limited speed range and controlled surfaces. Rough terrain, buoyancy control, and flexible caudal fins require further study before considering field applications.
